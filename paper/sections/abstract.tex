\textit{Objective.} EEG classification of Alzheimer's disease (AD) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD) on one
public dataset reports accuracies from about 50\% to 99\%, but studies change representation, graph, model and
evaluation protocol at once. We isolated representation and graph construction under a fixed graph neural
network, and tested a blood--brain-barrier-linked EEG marker.
\textit{Approach.} On OpenNeuro ds004504 (36 AD, 23 FTD, 29 controls) we crossed seven node-feature
representations with three graph constructions under one compact, untuned graph convolutional network, on 25
frozen subject-level splits and 25 confirmation splits, with matched baselines, 16 ablations, leakage and
permutation experiments, a simulation of the tests' calibration, external validation on a Parkinson's disease
cohort ($n=149$), and analysis of paroxysmal slow-wave events (PSWEs).
\textit{Main results.} Representation had larger effects than graph construction in every analysis. Uncorrected
and subject-level tests, however, flagged differences between equally informative representations in
\simCorrSubjLo--\simNullUncorr\% of simulated datasets; with a calibrated adjustment for overlapping training sets
the representation effect was significant externally ($p\nbExtRepP$) but not on ds004504 ($p\nbSelRepP$). Graph
construction had a negligible effect, averaged graph types were equivalent within $\pm\eqMargin$ macro-F1, and a
learned graph did no better. The best configuration (wavelets, spatial graph) reached macro-F1
\confFOne{} (95\% CI \confFOneCiLo{}--\confFOneCiHi{}); non-graph and deep-learning baselines did not differ
significantly. Window-level splitting raised macro-F1 to \cleakPThreespatialLeakySubj{}. PSWEs were more frequent
in AD (rate ratio \psweAdjADRR{}) and FTD (\psweAdjFTDRR{}) but not in Parkinson's disease; the excess tracked
background slowing ($\rho=\psweSlowRho$) and was not detectable among patients whose slowing overlapped with
controls'.
\textit{Significance.} The evaluation protocol moves reported performance far more than any modelling choice we
tested; with 88 subjects, differences between representations cannot be established once training-set overlap
is accounted for. Fixed-threshold PSWEs on scalp EEG mainly index background slowing. Code, folds and all raw
runs are released.
