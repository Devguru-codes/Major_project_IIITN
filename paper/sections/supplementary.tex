\subsection*{Supplementary methods}
\paragraph{Deep-learning baselines.} To place the GCN against standard end-to-end EEG networks, EEGNet-8,2
\cite{F14} (temporal convolution of 64 taps, depthwise spatial convolution, separable convolution;
\num{3331} parameters) and a compact 1D-CNN (three temporal convolution blocks over the 19 channels,
global average pooling; \num{47939} parameters) were trained on the same raw \SI{10}{\second} windows
as P5, with the same 25 selection splits, optimiser, early-stopping rule, subject-equal weighting and
subject-level aggregation as the GCN, so that every comparison is paired. EEGNet's max-norm weight
constraints were replaced by the shared weight decay; no hyperparameter was tuned. Because the original
EEGNet paper recommends dropout 0.25 for cross-subject classification, EEGNet was re-run once with that
value (everything else unchanged; \dropGpuHours{} GPU-hours) and compared with the original EEGNet and the
GCN in its own Holm family, so the pre-specified comparisons above are unaffected.

The GCN learns its weights from the training subjects only and uses the validation subjects only to
choose when to stop, whereas the classical models have no stopping rule. To compare models trained
on the same subjects, we evaluate both under two regimes on the same 25 test splits:
(i)~\emph{train only}, with the classical models fitted on the training subjects and the GCN as in the
main grid; and (ii)~\emph{train + val}, with the classical models fitted on training and validation
subjects and the GCN refitted on the same subjects for the number of epochs selected by early
stopping in the corresponding main-grid run (\nRefitRuns{} refits, no validation monitoring).

\paragraph{Severity stratification.} To ask whether the models only find advanced disease, patients were split
at the conventional mild cut-point (MMSE $\ge$ \sevMildMin{} versus $\le$ 20; MMSE is used for analysis only).
For P3~$\times$~spatial on both fold sets we compared, within AD and within FTD, the probability assigned to
the true class between strata (Mann--Whitney) and its rank correlation with MMSE (Spearman), with Holm
correction over the \sevNTests{} tests; the stratum sizes are those of the cohort and were not chosen.

\paragraph{Stopping-rule sensitivity.} Early stopping on about 14 validation subjects often selected
an early epoch. To test whether this shaped the conclusions, the full $7\times3$ grid was re-run on
the 25 selection splits with patience 50 instead of 20, and with no early stopping at all (100
epochs on the training subjects, final weights kept; validation subjects unused), each paired with
the main grid split by split.

\paragraph{Background-relative PSWE (exploratory).} After observing that the fixed-threshold rate
was strongly correlated with background slowing (Section~\ref{sec:res-pswe}), we added a
post-hoc analysis, labelled as such throughout. Its definitions were fixed in the configuration
before it was run on any real data. A relative PSWE is a run of at least \SI{5}{\second} in which a
channel's MPF lies below that channel's own recording-median MPF by at least \SI{2}{\hertz}
(primary), \SI{1.5}{\hertz} or \SI{3}{\hertz}, or by at least 2 or 3 robust standard deviations
($1.4826\times$ median absolute deviation).

\paragraph{Detector robustness.} Two properties of the fixed-threshold detector could bias it: with
\SI{2}{\second} windows the MPF is quantised to \SI{0.5}{\hertz} steps, so the \SI{6}{\hertz} threshold
falls on a bin edge; and artefacts add low-frequency power. In a pre-specified robustness analysis we
re-detected events with each segment zero-padded to an \SI{8}{\second} FFT (MPF on a
\SI{0.125}{\hertz} grid, window unchanged), with amplitude-based artefact rejection (a channel's
\SI{2}{\second} segment rejected if its peak-to-peak amplitude exceeded \SI{200}{\micro\volt} or it was
flat, SD $<\SI{0.5}{\micro\volt}$; rejected seconds break events and are removed from the exposure
time), and with both (primary), and repeated the statistics below. The unmodified variant reproduced
the main event counts exactly in both cohorts. The fixed-threshold statistics were also repeated on
the recordings preprocessed without ICA (A8), and across a grid of event rules (A11: MPF thresholds of 5, 6
and \SI{7}{\hertz} crossed with minimum durations of 3, 5 and \SI{8}{\second}).

\paragraph{Collinearity of the slowing adjustment.} Because PSWE rate and relative delta+theta power are
strongly correlated, a model containing both can give unstable group coefficients. We therefore report
the variance inflation of the slowing covariate given group, age and sex; refit the models on subjects
whose slowing lies within the range observed in controls (common support); and compare groups on the
ratio of observed to expected events, where the expectation comes from a negative-binomial model of
events on slowing, age and sex fitted in controls only.

\paragraph{Compute.}
All experiments ran on free Kaggle sessions (4 vCPUs); the only GPU use was the two deep-learning
baselines (\deepGpuHours{} GPU-hours of training on one Kaggle GPU, plus \dropGpuHours{} for the EEGNet
dropout sensitivity run). The 525-run main grid
took \cpuHoursGrid{} CPU-hours, and the \nConfirmRuns{} further runs (confirmation grid, ablations and permutations on the
confirmation folds, and the train + val refits) took \cpuHoursConfirm{} CPU-hours. The stopping-rule sensitivity grid and the
external cohort added \nExtraRuns{} runs and \cpuHoursExtra{} CPU-hours, and the learned-adjacency GCN
\learnNRuns{} runs and \cpuHoursLearned{} CPU-hours. Re-fitting the best cell for the occlusion analysis and
for its reproducibility check took 50 further training runs (about \cpuHoursOcc{} CPU-hours, estimated from the
main-grid run times of that cell). The mixed models, equivalence and adjusted omnibus tests, severity
analysis and calibration simulation (NB13--NB20) are statistical computations that took minutes each. Every run is logged with its configuration hash, code version and seed.

\subsection*{Supplementary results}
\paragraph{By-subject mixed model (descriptive).} In the by-subject mixed model (Table~\ref{tab:mixed}, Figure~\ref{fig:mixed}), representation
had a strong effect on both fold sets (selection $F(\mixSelRepDf,\mixSelRepDenDf)=\mixSelRepF$,
$p\mixSelRepP$; confirmation $F=\mixConfRepF$, $p\mixConfRepP$). The marginal mean probability assigned
to a subject's true class ranged from \mixSelMPTwo{} (P2) and \mixSelMPFour{} (P4) to \mixSelMPSix{} (P6)
and \mixSelMPThree{} (P3), and \mixSelNPairsSig{} of the 21 representation pairs differed after Holm
correction on each fold set. Graph construction and its interaction with representation were also
detectable at the subject level (selection: graph $F(\mixSelEdgeDf,\mixSelEdgeDenDf)=\mixSelEdgeF$,
$p\mixSelEdgeP$; interaction $F(\mixSelInterDf,\mixSelInterDenDf)=\mixSelInterF$, $p\mixSelInterP$;
confirmation: $p\mixConfEdgeP$ and $p\mixConfInterP$) but negligible in size: the three graph types'
marginal means spanned only \mixSelEdgeRange{} (\mixSelMspatial{} spatial, \mixSelMfunctional{} functional,
\mixSelMhybrid{} hybrid). Subjects accounted for \mixSelShareSubject\% of the variance and
subject~$\times$~representation for \mixSelShareSubjectxrepresentation\%, while subject~$\times$~graph
variance was estimated at zero: individuals differ in which representation suits them, not in which
graph does. A by-subject repeated-measures ANOVA on the same values gave essentially the same statistics
(representation $F=\mixSelAovRepF$, graph $F=\mixSelAovEdgeF$; Greenhouse--Geisser $p\mixSelAovRepP$ and
$p\mixSelAovEdgeP$). Modelling whether each subject was correctly classified, rather than the probability,
weakened every effect but left the pattern intact: representation remained significant on both fold sets
($F=\mixSelCorrRepF$, $p\mixSelCorrRepP$; confirmation $F=\mixConfCorrRepF$, $p\mixConfCorrRepP$), with
P3 again highest (mean proportion correct \mixSelCorrMPThree{} and \mixConfCorrMPThree{}), whereas the graph
effect was marginal on the selection folds ($p\mixSelCorrEdgeP$, range \mixSelCorrEdgeRange{}) and absent on
the confirmation folds ($p\mixConfCorrEdgeP$).

\paragraph{Stopping-rule sensitivity.} Early stopping selected a median of epoch \bestEpochMedian{}. Re-running the whole grid with patience
50 (median epoch \esPFiftyEpochMedian{}) or with no early stopping (100 epochs, final weights) left the
conclusions unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:es}, Figure~\ref{fig:es}): \bestCell{} remained the best cell
under every rule (\esPFiftyBestFOne{} and \esFixedBestFOne{}), the mean over all cells moved by at most
\esGrandShiftMax{}, cell rankings were preserved (Spearman $\rho=\esPFiftyRho$ and \esFixedRho{} against the main
grid), representation stayed significant in the uncorrected analysis, and no representation's score
changed significantly under
either rule (\esNSigWil{} of 14 Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests). Raw windows (P5), the configuration
with most parameters, did not benefit from longer training (\esPFiftyPFiveDelta{} and
\esFixedPFiveDelta{}), and no GCN-versus-classical comparison became significant. Formal equivalence
within $\pm\eqMargin$ was shown for \eqEsNEquiv{} of the \eqEsN{} representation-level comparisons, all
for patience 50; training without early stopping changed scores too variably for equivalence to be
shown.

\paragraph{Severity.} Mild patients were not detectably harder to classify. For \bestCell{}, mild AD
(MMSE $\ge$ \sevMildMin{}, $n=\sevSelADMildN$) was classified correctly in \sevSelADMildCorrect{} of runs on the
selection folds and \sevConfADMildCorrect{} on the confirmation folds, against \sevSelADModCorrect{} and
\sevConfADModCorrect{} for moderate-to-severe AD ($n=\sevSelADModN$); FTD showed the opposite, small difference
(\sevSelFTDMildCorrect{} mild, $n=\sevSelFTDMildN$, against \sevSelFTDModCorrect{}, $n=\sevSelFTDModN$;
Table~\ref{tab:severity}). None of the \sevNTests{} tests was significant (smallest uncorrected $p\sevPRawMin$),
and within patients the true-class probability was unrelated to MMSE ($|\rho|\le\sevRhoMaxAbs$). Mild AD was
less often correct than moderate-to-severe AD for \sevRepADNMildWorse{} of \sevRepADNReps{} representations,
so a modest disadvantage for early AD is plausible, but with only \sevSelADMildN{} mild AD patients it cannot be estimated.

\paragraph{Background-relative PSWEs (exploratory).} Defining PSWEs relative to each channel's own
background reversed the group pattern. With the primary definition (a drop of at least \SI{2}{\hertz}
below the channel median), AD showed \emph{fewer} transient slowing events than controls (rate ratio
\reldropTwoAdjADRR{}, \reldropTwoAdjADLo{}--\reldropTwoAdjADHi{}, $p\reldropTwoAdjADP$), FTD a
non-significant reduction (\reldropTwoAdjFTDRR{}), and the event rate was only weakly related to
slowing ($\rho=\reldropTwoSlowRho$) and not to MMSE ($\rho=\reldropTwoMmseRho$). After additional
adjustment for background MPF the AD effect was no longer significant (\reldropTwoAdjBgADRR{},
$p\reldropTwoAdjBgADP$). The MAD-scaled variants gave the same direction more strongly and survived
that adjustment (for 2~MAD: AD \relmadTwoAdjBgADRR{}, $p\relmadTwoAdjBgADP$; FTD \relmadTwoAdjBgFTDRR{},
$p\relmadTwoAdjBgFTDP$). Every variant pointed the same way: relative to their own background,
patients with dementia had fewer, not more, transient slowing events.
