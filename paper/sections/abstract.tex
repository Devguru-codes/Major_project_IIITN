\textit{Objective.} EEG-based classification of Alzheimer's disease (AD) and frontotemporal dementia
(FTD) on the same public dataset reports accuracies from 80\% to 99\%, but studies change the signal
representation, graph construction, model and evaluation protocol at once. We isolated these factors
and tested a blood--brain-barrier (BBB)-associated EEG marker.
\textit{Approach.} Using OpenNeuro ds004504 (36 AD, 23 FTD, 29 controls), we crossed seven node-feature
representations with three graph constructions under one fixed two-layer graph convolutional network,
evaluated on 25 frozen subject-level cross-validation splits (\nMainRuns{} runs) and re-evaluated on 25
independent confirmation splits, with a by-subject mixed model as the primary analysis, matched
non-graph baselines, 16 ablations, label permutations, a leakage experiment, stopping-rule sensitivity,
and external validation of the whole protocol on a Parkinson's disease cohort (ds004584, 149
participants). We also characterised paroxysmal slow-wave events (PSWEs), an EEG marker linked to BBB
dysfunction.
\textit{Main results.} Representation dominated ($F(\mixSelRepDf,\mixSelRepDenDf)=\mixSelRepF$, replicated at
\mixConfRepF{} on independent splits and \mixExtRepF{} externally); graph construction mattered little,
with graph types differing by less than 0.01 in the probability assigned to the true class, in a
direction that differed between cohorts. The best configuration (wavelet features, spatial graph)
reached macro-F1 \confFOne{} on the confirmation splits (95\% CI \confFOneCiLo{}--\confFOneCiHi{};
permutation $p=\cpermPThreespatialP$), not significantly better than non-graph classifiers; window-level
splitting of the identical pipeline raised it to \cleakPThreespatialLeakySubj{}. Externally the same
configuration reached \extSelFOne{}, leakage again inflated it (\extLeakySubj{}), but the ranking of
representations did not transfer ($\rho=\extRepsRho$). Fixed-threshold PSWEs were more frequent in AD
(rate ratio \psweAdjADRR{}) and FTD (\psweAdjFTDRR{}) but not in Parkinson's disease (\extPsweAdjRR{}); in
both cohorts the rate tracked background slowing ($\rho=\psweSlowRho$ and \extPsweSlowRho{}), robustly to
spectral zero-padding and artefact rejection.
\textit{Significance.} The evaluation protocol and the signal representation, not the graph or the
model, determine reported performance; representation rankings are disease-specific; and an EEG marker
of BBB dysfunction must be defined relative to background slowing. Code, frozen folds and all raw runs
are released.
